# Chapter 07 - Compliance and Claim Safety

Strong affiliate marketing is not only about persuasive copy.

It is also about disciplined copy.

If the claim structure is sloppy, three bad things happen:

- trust drops
- approval risk rises
- good affiliates stop wanting to promote the offer repeatedly

Trap Defence BTC becomes easier to promote when the compliance line is clear.

This chapter explains what must remain accurate, what can be used carefully, and what should not be used at all.

---

## 1. Why This Matters

For WarriorPlus affiliates, compliance is not a side issue.

It directly affects:

- platform safety
- audience trust
- long-term promotability
- refund and complaint risk
- whether the offer feels real or reckless

A defense-first offer should sound disciplined.

If the marketing starts sounding like exaggerated crypto hype, the copy stops matching the product.

That mismatch damages conversion quality even before it creates policy risk.

---

## 2. The Non-Negotiable Operating Truths

Every public-facing statement must remain consistent with the actual operating flow.

At the time of this guidebook draft, the core truths are:

- the sales page functions as an LP layer
- the actual purchase completes on WarriorPlus
- there is a single checkout path
- delivery is tied to WarriorPlus payment only
- access is fulfilled after purchase through the current delivery process

For practical messaging, that means:

- do not say checkout happens on Whop
- do not imply there are multiple payment routes
- do not imply buyers receive delivery before WarriorPlus payment
- do not imply some alternate gateway unlocks the same access

If affiliates build bridge pages, custom posts, or email copy, those statements must stay aligned with the approved flow.

---

## 3. The Approved Compliance Posture

The safest posture for this offer is:

- educational
- defense-first
- decision-support oriented
- non-guaranteed
- non-hype

The language should consistently imply:

- the product helps users read risk conditions better
- the product helps users avoid trap conditions
- the product provides context, not certainty
- the final decision remains with the user

In plain English, the posture is:

**This is a defense system for better judgment, not a guarantee machine.**

## 4. What Must Stay Accurate in Public Copy

The following ideas are safe only when they remain accurate and current.

### Sales Flow

Keep these accurate:

- purchase completes on WarriorPlus
- delivery follows WarriorPlus payment
- the offer routes into the current Telegram access experience

### Product Identity

Keep these accurate:

- Trap Defence BTC is defense-first
- `Minimal` is the entry layer
- `Regular` is the core paid offer
- `KIBA` is a paid-only value layer inside `Regular`

### Positioning Language

Keep these accurate:

- the offer is about avoiding traps, not chasing hype
- the philosophy includes waiting for clearer advantage
- the product uses structured market interpretation language such as `Trap Score`, `AVOID`, and `STANDBY`

If a statement makes the product sound materially different from the real experience, do not use it.

## 5. Green-Light Claims

These are the safest types of claims to use in public-facing affiliate content.

### Safe Operational Claims

These are generally safe because they describe the structure of the offer, not a promised outcome.

Examples:

- Trap Defence BTC is built around defense, not hype.
- `Minimal` is the free entry layer.
- `Regular` is the main paid defense environment.
- `KIBA` is available only inside the paid layer.
- The offer is available across six language lanes.

### Safe Positioning Claims

These are generally safe because they describe philosophy and approach.

Examples:

- Most traders lose by reacting too fast.
- The real edge is often knowing when not to act.
- Read structure, not noise.
- No hype. No signals. Just defense.
- This is a shield, not a gamble.

### Safe Mechanism Claims

These are generally safe because they describe how the product thinks, not what it guarantees.

Examples:

- Trap Score helps frame risk conditions.
- The product uses structured market context to help users think more clearly.
- KIBA is additional decision material inside the paid layer.
- The product is designed to help users avoid obvious trap conditions.

These are the claims you should build most public copy around.

---

## 6. Yellow-Light Claims

These are not automatically forbidden.

But they require caution, qualification, or current verification before public use.

### Testimonials and User Stories

Testimonials may be useful, but they must stay in the lane of testimony.

That means:

- present them as a user statement, not a guaranteed result
- do not turn one person’s outcome into a general promise
- avoid using dramatic examples as if they are audited proof

Safer format:

> One user described Trap Score as helping them stay out of a bad trade.

Less safe format:

> Trap Score will save you from five-figure losses.

### Affiliate Success Anecdotes

Claims like:

- first sale within hours
- seven conversions in one week
- higher conversion rate
- lower churn

should not be used publicly as hard proof unless they are verified, current, and approved for use.

If used at all, they should be framed as anecdotal and internal, not universal.

### AI Review Scores

If you use AI-review language, be careful.

It may be useful as design commentary, but it is not the same as market performance proof.

Do not imply that:

- AI scores prove returns
- AI review equals market validation
- AI commentary is independent commercial proof unless that is explicitly established

### Price, Trial, and Offer Terms

Old project materials contain outdated terms, including:

- coupon references
- trial references
- older referral flow wording
- older platform wording

Do not publish price-sensitive or offer-sensitive claims unless they are confirmed current for the WarriorPlus offer being promoted.

### Performance Narratives

Statements like these require caution:

- one trader gets wiped, another wakes up profitable
- saved me from a major loss
- this lowered churn
- this converts better

These may be useful as narrative framing, but not as hard proof unless properly supported.

## 7. Red-Light Claims

These should not be used in public-facing WarriorPlus affiliate copy.

### Guaranteed Outcome Claims

Do not say:

- guaranteed profit
- guaranteed win rate
- never lose again
- perfect entries
- predictable profits
- this always works

### Signal-Room Fantasy Claims

Do not say:

- this is a magic signal engine
- this predicts every move
- follow this and you will win
- guaranteed reversal signal

### Unsupported Hard Numbers

Do not use unsupported public-facing claims such as:

- exact win-rate claims
- exact capital-saved claims
- exact conversion claims
- exact churn-improvement claims

unless those figures are currently verified, approved, and intentionally cleared for public use.

### Real-Time X Scan Claims

Do not claim:

- live X sentiment scanning
- real-time X scanning by Grok
- direct X feed analysis by Grok

Current project guidance is clear that Grok output should not be described as direct real-time X scanning.

### Outdated Offer Claims

Do not use legacy copy that includes:

- `DEFEND50`
- coupon-based closes
- outdated referral instructions
- old Whop checkout assumptions
- unconfirmed trial language

### Incorrect Purchase Flow Claims

Do not say:

- checkout happens on Whop
- Whop payment unlocks delivery
- multiple payment gateways give the same access
- access is delivered outside the approved WarriorPlus purchase flow

These are direct accuracy risks.

### Mandatory delivery line when describing the flow

When you describe how the buyer gets access, use this line (or keep the meaning identical):

**Purchase completes on WarriorPlus. Access is delivered by email after payment.**

### Forbidden phrases — do not use (any language)

Do not use these or equivalent phrases in public affiliate copy, in any language:

- **English:** guaranteed profits, sure profits, sure wins, passive income, never lose, always win, get rich, money machine, printing money
- **Spanish:** ganancias garantizadas, ingresos pasivos seguros, ganar siempre
- **Portuguese:** lucros garantidos, renda passiva garantida
- **Arabic:** أرباح مضمونة، دخل سلبي مضمون
- **Korean:** 보장된 수익, 패시브 수입, 절대 손실 없음
- **Japanese:** 絶対勝てる, 必ず儲かる, 損しない, 保証された利益, 不労所得

If a phrase in your language implies guaranteed returns, instant wealth, or zero risk, do not use it. When in doubt, use the safe positioning from Section 5 (Green-Light Claims).

## 8. KIBA Language Rules

KIBA needs special handling because it is easy to oversell.

### Safe KIBA Language

Use language like:

- KIBA is a paid-only layer inside `Regular`
- KIBA adds a faster pulse to the paid environment
- KIBA should be treated as supplementary decision material
- KIBA helps serious users monitor potential structural shifts more closely

### Unsafe KIBA Language

Avoid language like:

- KIBA tells you exactly when to buy
- KIBA guarantees reversals
- KIBA is a direct money-printing signal
- KIBA alone tells you what trade to take

The safest principle is simple:

**KIBA can strengthen judgment, but it should not be marketed as guaranteed trade instruction.**

---

## 9. X Sentiment Language Rules

This is another area where public copy can drift into dangerous wording.

The safe position is:

- Grok-style narrative output is part of the interpretive layer
- it is not a direct claim of real-time X platform scanning

Safer language:

- narrative context
- sentiment-style interpretation
- market psychology layer
- language generation based on provided context

Less safe language:

- live X sentiment scan
- direct real-time X read
- Grok watches X and detects market mood live

Do not blur that distinction.

---

## 10. Disclaimer Discipline

Where a disclaimer is required, do not improvise casually.

The operating rule should be:

- use the approved WarriorPlus disclaimer wording where required
- keep it readable
- place it near the purchase action when used on sales pages or bridge pages
- do not hide it in tiny or hard-to-see formatting

For general affiliate content, the safest supporting posture is:

- educational purposes
- not financial advice
- decision-support, not guaranteed result

That tone should be consistent even when a full formal disclaimer is not being reproduced.

## 11. Legacy Asset Warning

The project contains a large amount of useful historical asset material.

That library is valuable.
But it is not plug-and-play for WarriorPlus v1.0.

Many legacy assets include one or more of the following:

- coupon references
- older trial language
- referral-link language tied to old systems
- stronger-than-safe proof language
- outdated platform wording
- risky phrasing around profitability

That means one simple rule:

**Treat legacy assets as source material, not final copy.**

Before reusing any legacy asset, sanitize it.

At minimum, remove or re-check:

- `DEFEND50`
- any discount language
- any unconfirmed trial language
- any `Whop checkout` language
- any old referral attribution wording
- any unsupported performance numbers
- any implication of guaranteed profitability

## 12. Safe vs Unsafe Examples

Here are practical examples of how to keep the offer strong without creating unnecessary risk.

### Example 1

Unsafe:

> KIBA predicts reversals before they happen, so you can profit first.

Safer:

> KIBA is a paid-only layer inside Regular that gives users additional context around potential structural shifts.

### Example 2

Unsafe:

> Grok scans X in real time and tells you exactly what the crowd is thinking.

Safer:

> The product includes a narrative and psychology layer designed to help users read market conditions more calmly.

### Example 3

Unsafe:

> Use Trap Defence BTC and stop losing money forever.

Safer:

> Trap Defence BTC is built to help users avoid obvious trap conditions and make calmer decisions.

### Example 4

Unsafe:

> Buy through Whop and get instant access.

Safer:

> Purchase completes on WarriorPlus, and delivery follows the approved access flow after payment.

### Example 5

Unsafe:

> This affiliate made seven sales in one week, so you can too.

Safer:

> Some affiliates have found the defense-first angle easier to promote than hype-based offers.

## 13. The Pre-Publish Safety Checklist

Before any affiliate-facing copy goes live, check the following:

1. Is the purchase flow described accurately?
2. Does the message avoid financial-advice framing?
3. Does the copy avoid guaranteed outcomes?
4. Is any testimonial clearly a testimonial, not a universal promise?
5. Are all numbers current, verified, and approved?
6. Has all legacy coupon or outdated flow language been removed?
7. Is `KIBA` positioned as support material, not a magic signal box?
8. Is any mention of X sentiment phrased carefully and truthfully?
9. If a disclaimer is needed, is the approved wording used correctly?

If any answer is no, revise the copy before using it.

---

## 14. The One-Sentence Summary

The compliance standard for Trap Defence BTC is simple:

**stay accurate, stay disciplined, and market the offer as a defense system that improves judgment without promising certainty.**

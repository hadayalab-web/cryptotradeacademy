# Chapter 12 - 7-Day Launch Plan

This chapter turns the guidebook into action.

The goal is not to build a perfect affiliate machine in one week.

The goal is to:

- start moving
- test the right message
- send the right traffic to the right step
- learn which audience responds fastest

This launch plan assumes you are starting from zero or close to zero.

---

## 1. Core Launch Rule

Do not spend seven days preparing without publishing.

The first week should be about live signal gathering, not private overthinking.

Your working model for the week is:

`publish -> observe -> reply -> DM -> route -> refine`

Keep the funnel simple:

- cold traffic -> `Minimal`
- warm traffic -> paid explanation
- hot traffic -> WarriorPlus link

---

## 2. What You Need Before Day 1

Before starting the plan, prepare:

- your `Minimal` link
- your WarriorPlus affiliate link
- one or two short hooks
- one three-line post
- one short DM template
- one short video script if possible

If you already have those, you are ready enough.

Do not delay the launch waiting for perfection.

---

## 3. Success Criteria for Week One

Your first-week goal is not huge scale.

It is proof of motion.

At the end of the seven days, you want:

- several public posts published
- a batch of high-intent replies sent
- at least a few warm DM conversations started
- real feedback on which pain angle gets the best reaction
- a clearer sense of which audience lane responds best

That is enough to make week two smarter.

---

## 4. Day 1 - Setup and First Signal

Objective:

Get live immediately and remove setup friction.

Actions:

1. Save your `Minimal` link and paid link.
2. Pick one buyer pain to focus on first.
3. Pick one language lane.
4. Publish one clean public post.
5. Prepare one DM opener and one reply template.

Best Focus:

Use one of these pain angles:

- whale traps
- overtrading
- chart exhaustion
- reacting too fast

Best CTA:

Use:

- `Minimal`

End-of-Day Check:

Ask:

- did I publish at least one asset?
- do I know which pain I am targeting tomorrow?

---

## 5. Day 2 - Intent Capture Through Replies

Objective:

Move from broad visibility into higher-intent conversations.

Actions:

1. Find recent posts that show pain, confusion, or loss.
2. Send a focused batch of relevant replies.
3. Use short, natural reply openers.
4. Route interested people to `Minimal`.
5. Note which reply angle gets the best reaction.

Best Targets:

Reply to people showing:

- liquidation pain
- FOMO
- stop-loss frustration
- market confusion

Best CTA:

Still use:

- `Minimal`

End-of-Day Check:

Ask:

- which reply style got more attention?
- which pain type felt hottest?

---

## 6. Day 3 - Public Repetition and Profile Alignment

Objective:

Make the message feel consistent wherever people look.

Actions:

1. Publish another public post using a different angle.
2. Update bio or pinned content if needed.
3. Make sure your profile points clearly to the free layer.
4. Reuse the same core positioning words:
   defense, structure, trap avoidance, restraint

Best Angle Rotation:

If day 1 was whale-trap pain, use one of these next:

- read structure, not noise
- 70% of the time, do nothing
- no hype. no signals. just defense

End-of-Day Check:

Ask:

- does my profile support the same message as my posts?
- if someone saw one reply and then my profile, would the story still make sense?

---

## 7. Day 4 - Warm DMs and Early Paid Transition

Objective:

Start deeper conversations with people who already showed interest.

Actions:

1. Follow up people who engaged on days 1-3.
2. Use short DMs, not giant paragraphs.
3. Send `Minimal` first if they are still early.
4. Explain `Regular` only when the person asks what the full version is.
5. Mention `KIBA` only after `Regular` is clear.

Best DM Structure:

Use:

- pain acknowledgment
- defense reframe
- free-layer or paid-layer route

End-of-Day Check:

Ask:

- who is still cold?
- who is warm?
- who is asking paid questions?

Sort people by temperature.

---

## 8. Day 5 - Short Video or High-Contrast Asset

Objective:

Increase low-friction reach with a format that hits quickly.

Actions:

1. Publish one short video or one short high-contrast asset.
2. Keep the first-second hook strong.
3. Use simple emotional contrast:
   trapped vs protected, noise vs structure, speed vs restraint
4. Route to `Minimal`.

Good Video Logic:

The best video tells one simple story:

- most traders lose by walking into traps
- the difference is seeing the trap earlier
- the first step is the free layer

End-of-Day Check:

Ask:

- did video create comments, profile visits, or clicks?
- did it bring in a different kind of audience than replies did?

---

## 9. Day 6 - Market-Day Leverage

Objective:

Use visible BTC movement to make the offer more relevant.

Actions:

1. Watch for a sharp move, confusion spike, or visible emotional market day.
2. Publish a structure-first post.
3. Send a batch of relevant replies while the emotion is fresh.
4. If the audience is warm enough, use a sanitized `Regular` swipe.
5. Do not overdo the hard close.

Why This Day Matters:

The offer becomes more vivid when the market itself is noisy.

This is when:

- trap language
- defense language
- wait-for-clarity language

often lands harder.

End-of-Day Check:

Ask:

- did the market create better response quality?
- did people respond more to pain or to structure?

---

## 10. Day 7 - Review and Double Down

Objective:

End week one by learning, not guessing.

Actions:

1. Review which channel created the best response quality.
2. Review which pain angle got the best reaction.
3. Review where warm conversations began.
4. Review which CTA worked best for which audience temperature.
5. Pick one channel and one angle to push harder next week.

Questions to Answer:

- Which channel brought the best-quality attention?
- Which angle got the strongest emotional response?
- Which people moved from public engagement into DM?
- Which conversations reached the paid-explanation stage?
- Where did the funnel feel smooth, and where did it stall?

The Main Output of Day 7:

By the end of this day, you should know:

- your best first channel
- your best first message
- your best first audience pain

That is enough to make week two more efficient.

## 11. The Weekly Cadence in One View

| Day | Main Job | Primary Channel | Primary CTA |
| ------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| 1 | Setup + first live post | X public post | `Minimal` |
| 2 | Capture intent | X replies | `Minimal` |
| 3 | Strengthen public consistency | X public + profile | `Minimal` |
| 4 | Warm follow-up | DMs | `Minimal` or paid explanation |
| 5 | Low-friction reach | Short video | `Minimal` |
| 6 | Use live market context | X post + replies | `Minimal` or sanitized paid mention |
| 7 | Review + double down | Internal review | next-week priority |

---

## 12. Metrics That Matter in Week One

Do not obsess over perfect conversion data in the first seven days.

Instead, track:

- public post response quality
- reply engagement
- profile visits
- clicks into `Minimal`
- DMs started
- warm paid questions

These are the right early indicators.

Week one is about signal quality, not vanity volume.

---

## 13. Common Week-One Mistakes

Avoid these:

- waiting too long before first publish
- changing the core message every day
- using the paid link too early
- trying to explain the whole product stack in every message
- using old unsanitized coupon copy
- introducing `KIBA` before `Regular` is clear
- measuring discovery channels like direct-conversion channels

---

## 14. What Week Two Should Look Like

If week one goes correctly, week two should not start from scratch.

Week two should do more of what already showed signs of working.

That means:

- repeat the strongest angle
- lean harder into the strongest channel
- improve the handoff from public engagement to DM
- keep routing cold traffic to `Minimal`
- make paid explanations clearer for warm leads

This is how the funnel compounds.

---

## 15. The One-Sentence Summary

The first seven days should be used to find your best pain angle, your best first channel, and your cleanest path from `Minimal` into `Regular`.
